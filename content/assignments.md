## The idea behind the sequence

Each assessment is a **handover**. Homework 1 hands your work to another person.
Homework 2 hands it to another environment. The
[group project](#/project) hands it to a user.

<div class="key">
Everything in this course is graded on whether the handover succeeds — not on
whether it ran once, on your machine, on the day you wrote it.
</div>

## Homework 1 — human handover

**Individual · 25% · after the early block**

> Can another person understand and reproduce your work?

The focus is code literacy and reproducibility. You work with code, explain what
it does, find and fix what is wrong with it, and package the result so that a
reader who has never seen it can run it and get the same answer.

It draws on:

- **code literacy** — reading, explaining, modifying and debugging;
- **reproducibility** — inputs, instructions, dependencies, a fresh run;
- **project structure and Git** — a history someone can follow;
- **debugging** — finding the cause, not just making the error disappear;
- **tests and checks** — evidence that the result is right;
- **documentation** — a README that actually lets someone start;
- **responsible AI use** — assistance you can account for.

There may be a **peer-review / reproduction component**, in which another
student attempts to reproduce your work from what you supplied.

<div class="warn">
Exact tasks, deadlines, submission route and the peer-review format are
announced on Moodle. They are not final yet and are deliberately not guessed
at here.
</div>

## Homework 2 — technical handover

**Individual · 25% · after the later technical blocks**

> Can the workflow run reliably outside your own setup?

The second homework moves along the course map, from data through engineering to
production. It typically involves relational data and SQL, validating what the
data means, structuring code into something modular and testable, and fixing the
environment so the work runs somewhere other than your laptop — with Docker where
that is appropriate.

<div class="warn">
As with Homework 1, the exact requirements, datasets and deadlines come through
Moodle once they are finalised.
</div>

## Working with AI on homework

AI assistance is **permitted and encouraged, according to the task** — see
[AI &amp; responsible use](#/ai) for what that means in practice. The short
version: you remain responsible for what you submit, you must be able to explain
it, and important outputs must be checked independently.
