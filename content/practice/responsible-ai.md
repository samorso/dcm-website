## Learning objectives

Generative AI can assist with reading, programming and data analysis. A useful output does not by itself establish that the tool was appropriate or the work reliable. By the end of this activity, you should be able to:

1. Recognize key scientific-integrity principles and recommendations for responsible AI use at UNIL.
2. Assess when information may be processed with different families of AI tools.
3. Distinguish data-related authorization from permission to use AI in assessed coursework.
4. Evaluate AI-generated results and explain significant AI contributions appropriately.

**What to submit:** Only the Moodle quiz. You do not have to hand in your activity notes.  
**You need:** A browser and the two readings; no programming or paid AI account is necessary.  
**Safety:** Do not upload, paste or type real personal, confidential, employer or research-participant information into any AI service for this activity.

## Part 1 — Read and understand (25 minutes)

### Reading 1 — Scientific integrity and responsible AI (about 10 minutes)

[UNIL: Scientific integrity and responsible use of generative AI in the academic context](https://wp.unil.ch/iaunil/en/scientific-integrity-and-responsible-use-of-generative-ai-in-the-academic-context-at-unil/) (8 July 2025).

Read the article in full. Focus on the four principles of scientific integrity and the six recommendations for responsible use.

Consider: Who remains responsible when AI makes an error? What kinds of AI use should be disclosed? Which decisions must remain under human responsibility?

### Reading 2 — Choosing appropriate AI tools (about 15 minutes)

[UNIL: AI_GO — two minutes to find out which AI tools you are allowed to use with your data](https://wp.unil.ch/iaunil/en/ai_go-two-minutes-to-find-out-which-ai-tools-you-are-allowed-to-use-with-your-data/) (10 August 2026).

Focus on **Why this matters**, **The three families of AI tools**, **Five situations, five paths** (especially situations 1, 2 and 4), and **Good to know**.

Note: AI_GO evaluates data-related conditions. It does **not** grant permission to use AI in an examination, graded report or publication, and it does not override copyright, confidentiality or contractual restrictions.

## Part 2 — Investigate two scenarios (40 minutes)

Open [UNIL's AI_GO questionnaire](https://ia.unil.ch/AI_GO). Use **only the hypothetical information below** to explore each path. You do not need to upload a file or use an actual AI model.

### Scenario A — Academic report (15 minutes)

You are writing a graded report on sales forecasting. You have a scientific article freely available from a publisher website, but its reuse license is not specified. You have also created a small **entirely synthetic**, unrestricted sales dataset describing no real people.

You would like a commercial AI assistant to summarize the article, produce Python code for the synthetic dataset and explain the results.

**Your tasks:**

1. Use AI_GO for the synthetic dataset. Which families of tools does it suggest under the stated assumptions?
2. Does free access to the article necessarily authorize uploading its complete text to a commercial service?
3. If the dataset is unrestricted, does that automatically authorize using AI to complete the graded report?
4. If the generated code runs without errors, what additional checks would be useful?
5. If AI makes a significant contribution to your report, what would you disclose?

Write a private **3–5 sentence recommendation**. It is not submitted.

### Scenario B — Confidential employer data (15 minutes)

During an internship, your supervisor gives you a **confidential** spreadsheet containing employee codes (names removed), department, job title, age, office location and annual salary. The records may remain identifiable. No data-protection impact assessment has been carried out. Your employer has not authorized sending the data to external AI services or moving it to your personal laptop.

You want AI to clean the spreadsheet, summarize salaries and produce charts.

**Your tasks:**

1. Explore the identifiable-income-data situation in AI_GO. What tool family does the published example indicate without an impact assessment?
2. Does removing employee names necessarily anonymize the records?
3. Does choosing a local model automatically authorize use of the confidential employer data on your laptop?
4. What additional authorization or working environment might be needed?
5. Propose a way to develop and check the analysis without exposing the real data to an unauthorized tool.

Write a private **3–5 sentence recommendation**. It is not submitted.

### Challenge — Critique an AI recommendation (10 minutes)

An AI assistant tells you:

> “Employee names have been removed, so the salary spreadsheet is anonymous. Upload it to any commercial AI chatbot. Also, if AI_GO recommends an AI tool, that automatically means you may use it in a graded university assignment.”

Identify **at least three distinct problems** in this advice. Explain what you would do differently, using the UNIL readings to support your reasoning.
